## Tasleemat Forms Guide # Project Artifact: Release Plan **Document Reference:** `PMO-04.03.09` This document provides a comprehensive, professional reference to understand the purpose and effective usage of the **Release Plan** in alignment with the Tasleemat framework. --- ### 1. What? A formal Tasleemat-aligned project document known as the **Release Plan**, which is similar to a roadmap and functions as a high-level schedule showing which release each requirement or user story will be assigned to. The contents of a release can change according to the relative priority of the requirements in the backlog and the availability of the resources needed to work on them. The plan can be elaborated into sprints, several to a release, and once a sprint starts its contents cannot change. It is therefore a forecast rather than a commitment, and stays dynamic throughout the project as priorities shift and new requirements are identified. The form records a release table (release, dates, user stories, release goal, status) and a sprint breakdown (sprint, dates, goal, user stories, status). --- ### 2. Why? To give the customer and the team a shared view of what is coming and when, and to keep the release sequence honest as priorities shift, so that what is promised is traceable to a backlog identifier rather than to recollection. --- ### 3. When? This artifact is prepared at the **PLANNING** Process Group, under Schedule. A high-level plan may be developed once the product vision and the backlog have started, and it is re-sequenced after each sprint as priorities shift and new requirements are identified. --- ### 4. Who? **Responsibilities:** Maintained by the Product Owner with the Project Manager, reviewed after every sprint, and agreed with the customer for committed releases. the ordering and for its accuracy. Estimated, reviewed, and updated by the team. --- ### Tailoring Tips * As information in the release plan gets more concrete you may want to assign requirements or user stories to specific sprints in the release. * You can arrange the release plan in swim lanes, with each lane assigned to a specific team or work stream. * If you show the release plan with a timeline you can show the relationships between various user stories or features. This blends the information about the content of each release with the schedule information. --- ### Alignment & Dependencies #### 1. Pre-requisites & Inputs (Upstream Dependencies) * **Mandatory:** * Work Breakdown Structure / WBS (PMO-04.02.06) * Scope Statement (PMO-04.02.05) * **Optional / Contextual:** * Resource Requirements (PMO-04.06.02) * Risk Register (PMO-04.08.02) #### 2. Downstream Dependents * **Mandatory:** * Project Schedule Baseline (PMO-04.03.08) * Earned Value Analysis / EVA (PMO-06.05) * **Optional / Contextual:** * Release Plan (PMO-04.03.09) * Lookahead Planning Log (PMO-04.03.10) --- ### 5. How? To accurately and professionally complete the **RELEASE PLAN**, the responsible party must populate the following sections based on the project context (ensure `parameters.md` is referenced for global project variables): * **Release:** The release identifier and name, for example R1 or Release 1. A release is the unit the customer receives, not an internal milestone, so name it the way the customer would recognise it. * **Start Date:** When the release starts. For a high-level plan a month or a quarter is honest; a day-level date implies a precision the plan does not have. * **End Date:** When the release finishes. Give the duration as well as the end date, since the two together are what show whether the release is gaining or losing ground. * **User Stories:** The requirements or user stories from the backlog assigned to this release, referenced by their backlog identifier rather than restated, so the plan and the backlog cannot drift apart. * **Release Goal:** What this release is for, in one sentence. A release without a goal turns into a list of everything that happened to fit, which is how releases acquire unrelated work. * **Status:** Not started, in progress, or released. This is the column that makes the plan honest: once a sprint has started its contents cannot change, so anything still marked in progress is committed, and anything not started can still move. * **Sprint:** The sprint identifier and the release it belongs to, so the plan can be read per release rather than as one long list. * **Dates:** When the sprint starts and ends. A sprint with no dates cannot be told apart from one that is still open to negotiation, and once it has started its contents are fixed. * **Sprint Goal:** What this sprint is for, in one sentence. Several sprints toward one release goal is the normal case; a sprint goal that contradicts the release goal is a sign the release is carrying two unrelated pieces of work. * **Sprint User Stories:** The user stories committed to this sprint, by backlog identifier. Once the sprint has started this list is what was committed, and a change to it is a change to the commitment rather than to the plan. * **Sprint Status:** Not started, in progress, or complete. --- ### 📥 Associated Templates * [📄 Printable Template (Markdown)](../../../../forms/en/04_Planning/03_Schedule/04_03_09_Release_Plan_Template.html) * [🤖 LLM Generation Prompt](https://github.com/fakhruldeen/Tasleemat/blob/main/forms/en/04_Planning/03_Schedule/09_Release_Plan/04_03_09_Release_Plan.md) * [📊 Data Structure (JSON)](https://github.com/fakhruldeen/Tasleemat/blob/main/forms/en/04_Planning/03_Schedule/09_Release_Plan/04_03_09_Release_Plan.json) * [📈 Tabular Data (CSV)](https://github.com/fakhruldeen/Tasleemat/blob/main/forms/en/04_Planning/03_Schedule/09_Release_Plan/04_03_09_Release_Plan.csv) --- ### 6.
