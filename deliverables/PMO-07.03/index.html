<!--  LLM INSTRUCTIONS: Fill in the [ Add details... ] placeholders based on project context.

Project closeout involves documenting the final project performance as compared to the project objectives. The objectives from the project charter are reviewed and evidence of meeting them is documented. If an objective was not met, or if there is a variance, that is documented as well. In addition, information from the procurement closeout is documented.

This form supports process 4.7 Close Project or Phase in the PMBOK Guide - Sixth Edition.

Writing guidance:
*   This is the document the organization reads after the project team has been released, so write it for a reader who was not on the project. Do not assume context that is not written down.
*   Record objectives that were not met, and variances that were not in favour. A closeout that reports only successes is the one artifact the organization is most likely to over-trust.
*   Give the source of every piece of evidence, not the conclusion drawn from it. "Verified by test report TR-114" can be followed up; "verified" cannot.
*   Where something remained open at closure, name it and name who now owns it. An item transferred without a named recipient has not actually been transferred, and will be lost.
*   Cross-reference the product acceptance form, the lessons learned summary, and the contract closeout report rather than restating them, so that the records cannot drift apart.
*   A section that did not apply should say so in one line. Do not delete the section and do not pad it; a reader needs to know it was considered and found empty.

Alignment: this closeout should be aligned and consistent with the project management plan (all components), the product acceptance, and the lessons learned summary.

Tailoring Notes:
*   When working with an incremental life cycle or agile development method, the delivery of a major end item, service, or capability may benefit from a formal phase closeout report.
*   For projects that are part of a program, tailor the content to meet the needs of the program.
*   Use the information from your project to determine the best approach.

Section Instructions:
*   **Project Description:** Provide a summary-level description of the project or phase.
*   **Performance Summary:** For Scope and for Quality, state the objectives, the completion criteria that decide whether they were met, and the evidence that they were met. Take the objectives from the approved plan, not from recollection.

*   **Benefits Management:** Describe how the final product, service, or result achieved the benefits the project was undertaken to address.

What? A formal Tasleemat-aligned project document known as the **Project or Phase Closeout**, used to document the final project performance as compared to the project objectives. The objectives from the project charter are reviewed and evidence of meeting them is documented. Where an objective was not met, or where there is a variance, that is documented as well, together with the information from the procurement closeout. The Performance Summary is a matrix: Scope and Quality as rows, against Objectives, Completion Criteria, and How Met as columns. The Variances section is a second matrix: Time and Cost as rows, against Objectives, Final Outcome, Variance, and Comments. --- ### 2. Why? To give the organization a durable record of what the project delivered and what it cost to deliver it, so that decisions about future work rest on evidence rather than recollection, and so that nothing open at closure is silently lost. --- ### 3. When? This artifact is prepared at the **CLOSING** Process Group of the project lifecycle.
