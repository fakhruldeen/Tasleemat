*   **Process or Artifact:** What is being tailored, named specifically enough to be found in the methodology. "The risk process" is not a decision anyone can approve, because there are several and the one approved may not be the one implemented.

*   **Reversibility:** Whether the change can be undone, and what would trigger reversing it. Most tailoring is reversible in principle and irreversible in practice, because by the time the cost becomes clear the team has built its way around the change.

*   **Compliance Check:** How the plan is checked against the applicable standards, and who performs the check. A self-check performed by the party that wanted the change is not a check.

## Tasleemat Forms Guide # Project Artifact: Tailoring Plan **Document Reference:** `PMO-02.01` This document provides a comprehensive, professional reference to understand the purpose and effective usage of the **Tailoring Plan** in alignment with the Tasleemat framework. --- ### 1. What? A baselined record of every departure from the organisational methodology, and of the decisions and standards the project operates under. It states what is being tailored and from which version of the method, what forces the change, which standards apply regardless, which tailoring is inherited from above, and for each decision what the default was, what replaces it, why, what it costs, and whether it can be undone. --- ### 2. Why? Because a methodology is a default rather than a law, and the value of having one is that everyone starts from the same place. Tailoring destroys that value unless it is recorded: an unrecorded departure is invisible to the next person who joins, to the assurance function, and to anyone reviewing the project afterwards, and it is always discovered late. Recording the default alongside the decision is what makes the departure legible, and recording the consequence is what makes it reviewable. --- ### 3. When? This artifact is primarily prepared, utilized, and updated during the **PROJECT APPROACH AND TAILORING Process Group** of the project lifecycle. It is baselined before execution begins and revisited whenever a decision is challenged by its consequences or the project's circumstances change. --- ### 4. Who? **Responsibilities:** Developed by the Project Manager with input from the project team and Subject Matter Experts, then baselined. Approved by the PMO lead, who is accountable for the consistency of the approach across the portfolio, and by the sponsor where a decision affects a standard the sponsor is required to uphold. --- ### Tailoring Tips * The plan may record only the decisions that differ from the methodology, provided the methodology version is stated, since a reader with the methodology in hand can derive the defaults. Recording the defaults explicitly is still preferable where the decision is one that will be challenged. * Inherited tailoring from programme level should be recorded rather than assumed, since most deviations a project finds are not its own and the project inherits responsibility for them whether it made them or not. * A small project may consolidate the basis and governance sections into a short preamble, but the decisions table should retain the default and the consequence, as those are the two columns that make a decision defensible. * Where a decision was made under time pressure, it is worth recording that explicitly, because an expedited decision reviewed later looks identical to one that was never challenged. * The plan may be superseded rather than amended after baseline, since a change to a baselined decision is a change to the plan and should be traceable as such. --- ### Alignment & Dependencies #### 1. TAILORING PLAN - LLM GENERATION GUIDE

System Prompt / Instructions: This document serves as the detailed instruction set for generating the TAILORING PLAN. When asked to populate this form, use the guidance provided for each section below to accurately generate the required content. Reference parameters.md for global project variables.

Context & Definition: Tailoring is the deliberate departure from a standard method, recorded as such. The distinction that matters is between a decision and a default: a methodology says what to do unless there is a reason not to, and tailoring is the part where the reason is written down. An unwritten deviation is not tailoring, it is a departure that will be discovered later by someone auditing the project, and by then the team has built its way around it.

The three sections follow the life of a tailoring decision. The basis establishes what is being tailored from, what forces a change, what cannot be traded away, and what the project has already inherited from above it. The decisions record each change with the default it departs from, because without the default a reader cannot tell whether a requirement was loosened or merely renamed. The governance section establishes who may approve what, and what would cause a decision to be reopened.

The field most often skipped is the consequence. Every tailoring has a cost, and a plan that records only what improves is a summary of intentions. Recording what becomes harder is what makes the decision reviewable by someone who was not in the conversation when it was made.

Alignment & Dependencies: * Pre-requisites (Inputs): * Mandatory: Project Charter (PMO-03.01), Organizational PMO Methodology * Optional: Program Charter (PMO-00.02), Risk Management Plan (PMO-04.08.01) * Downstream Dependents: * Mandatory: Project Management Plan (PMO-04.01.01), Quality Management Plan (PMO-04.05.01) * Optional: AI Governance Plan (PMO-02.02), Team Charter (PMO-04.06.05)
