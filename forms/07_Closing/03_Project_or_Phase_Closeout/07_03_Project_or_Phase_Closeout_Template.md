<!--  LLM INSTRUCTIONS: Fill in the [ Add details... ] placeholders based on project context.

Project closeout involves documenting the final project performance as compared to the project objectives. The objectives from the project charter are reviewed and evidence of meeting them is documented. If an objective was not met, or if there is a variance, that is documented as well. In addition, information from the procurement closeout is documented.

This form supports process 4.7 Close Project or Phase in the PMBOK Guide - Sixth Edition.

Writing guidance:
*   This is the document the organization reads after the project team has been released, so write it for a reader who was not on the project. Do not assume context that is not written down.
*   Record objectives that were not met, and variances that were not in favour. A closeout that reports only successes is the one artifact the organization is most likely to over-trust.
*   Give the source of every piece of evidence, not the conclusion drawn from it. "Verified by test report TR-114" can be followed; "verified" cannot.
*   Where something remained open at closure, name it and name who now owns it. An item transferred without a named recipient has not actually been transferred, and will be lost.
*   Cross-reference the product acceptance form, the lessons learned summary, and the contract closeout report rather than restating them, so that the records cannot drift apart.

Alignment: this closeout should be aligned and consistent with the project management plan (all components), the product acceptance, and the lessons learned summary.

Tailoring Notes:
*   When working with an incremental life cycle or agile development method, the delivery of a major end item, service, or capability may benefit from a formal phase closeout report.
*   For projects that are part of a program, tailor the content to meet the needs of the program.
*   Use the information from your project to determine the best approach.

Section Instructions:
*   **Project Description:**
    -   **Description:** Provide a summary-level description of the project or phase: what it was, its purpose, and its boundaries. Keep it to a summary, and point to the project charter for detail.
*   **Performance Summary:**
    -   **Scope Objectives:** Describe the scope objectives needed to achieve the planned benefits of the project.
    -   **Completion Criteria:** Document the specific and measurable criteria needed to complete the scope objectives. State each one so that it can be answered met or not met.
    -   **How Met:** Provide the evidence that the completion criteria were met. Give the source of the evidence, such as the document, test result, or acceptance record, not only the conclusion.
    -   **Quality Objectives:** Describe the quality objectives and criteria needed to achieve the planned benefits of the project.
    -   **Quality Criteria:** Document the specific and measurable criteria needed to meet the product and project quality objectives.
    -   **Verification and Validation:** Enter the verification and validation information from the product acceptance form. Cross-reference that form rather than restating it, so the two records cannot drift apart.
*   **Variances:**
    -   **Time Objectives:** The planned time objectives, being the approved schedule baseline duration and the planned completion date.
    -   **Final Completion Date:** The actual date the project or phase was completed.
    -   **Time Variance:** The difference between the planned and actual completion, and the reason for it. Where the project finished early, say so and explain why, since an unexplained favourable variance hides a planning error just as a delay does.
    -   **Cost Objectives:** The approved cost baseline, or the total budget at completion if no baseline applies.
    -   **Final Expenditures:** The actual total spent at completion, and whether it includes contingency drawn or reserve released.
    -   **Cost Variance:** The difference between the planned and actual cost, in currency and as a percentage, and the reason for it.
*   **Benefits Management:**
    -   **How Benefits Were Achieved:** Describe how the final product, service, or result achieved the benefits the project was undertaken to address. Identify the benefit and the evidence that it was realised, distinguishing a benefit that was realised from one that is still expected.
    -   **Benefits Realisation Status:** The current realisation status of each benefit, and who owns tracking it after the project team has been released.
*   **Business Needs:**
    -   **How Business Needs Were Met:** Describe how the final product, service, or result achieved the business needs identified in the business plan.
    -   **Needs Not Met:** Any business need that was not met, and what happens next now that the project is closed. A need left unstated here will be assumed met by whoever inherits the product.
*   **Risks and Issues:**
    -   **Significant Risks and Issues:** Summarize any significant risks or issues, or the overall risk exposure, and describe the response and resolution strategies used. Record what remained open at closure, because an unresolved item transferred silently is the one most often lost.
    -   **Items Transferred:** Any item transferred to the organization or to operations, with the recipient named. An item with no named recipient has not been transferred.
