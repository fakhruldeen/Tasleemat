## Tasleemat Forms Guide
# Project Artifact: Project or Phase Closeout

**Document Reference:** `PMO-07.03`

This document provides a comprehensive, professional reference to understand
the purpose and effective usage of the **Project or Phase Closeout** in
alignment with the Tasleemat framework.

---

### 1. What?
A formal Tasleemat-aligned project document known as the **Project or Phase
Closeout**, used to document the final project performance as compared to the
project objectives. The objectives from the project charter are reviewed and
evidence of meeting them is documented. Where an objective was not met, or
where there is a variance, that is documented as well, together with the
information from the procurement closeout.

---

### 2. Why?
To give the organization a durable record of what the project delivered and
what it cost to deliver it, so that decisions about future work rest on evidence
rather than recollection, and so that nothing open at closure is silently lost.

---

### 3. When?
This artifact is prepared at the **CLOSING** Process Group of the project
lifecycle. It supports process 4.7 Close Project or Phase, and is produced at
the close of a phase for long projects, and at the close of the project for
shorter ones.

---

### 4. Who?
**Responsibilities:** Compiled by the Project Manager, signed off by the Project
Sponsor or Client and the Operations or Service Owner who receives the product,
and archived with the project records.

---

### Tailoring Tips
*   When working with an incremental life cycle or agile development method,
    the delivery of a major end item, service, or capability may benefit from a
    formal phase closeout report.
*   For projects that are part of a program, tailor the content to meet the
    needs of the program.
*   Use the information from your project to determine the best approach.

---

### Alignment
This closeout should be aligned and consistent with the following documents:
*   Project management plan (all components)
*   Product acceptance
*   Lessons learned summary

---

### 5. How?
To accurately and professionally complete the **PROJECT OR PHASE CLOSEOUT**, the responsible party must
populate the following sections based on the project context (ensure
`parameters.md` is referenced for global project variables):

*   **Project Description / Description:** Provide a summary-level description of the project or phase: what it was, its purpose, and its boundaries. Keep it to a summary, and point to the project charter for detail.
*   **Performance Summary / Scope Objectives:** Describe the scope objectives needed to achieve the planned benefits of the project.
*   **Performance Summary / Completion Criteria:** Document the specific and measurable criteria needed to complete the scope objectives. State each one so that it can be answered met or not met.
*   **Performance Summary / How Met:** Provide the evidence that the completion criteria were met. Give the source of the evidence, such as the document, test result, or acceptance record, not only the conclusion.
*   **Performance Summary / Quality Objectives:** Describe the quality objectives and criteria needed to achieve the planned benefits of the project.
*   **Performance Summary / Quality Criteria:** Document the specific and measurable criteria needed to meet the product and project quality objectives.
*   **Performance Summary / Verification and Validation:** Enter the verification and validation information from the product acceptance form. Cross-reference that form rather than restating it, so the two records cannot drift apart.
*   **Variances / Time Objectives:** The planned time objectives, being the approved schedule baseline duration and the planned completion date.
*   **Variances / Final Completion Date:** The actual date the project or phase was completed.
*   **Variances / Time Variance:** The difference between the planned and actual completion, and the reason for it. Where the project finished early, say so and explain why, since an unexplained favourable variance hides a planning error just as a delay does.
*   **Variances / Cost Objectives:** The approved cost baseline, or the total budget at completion if no baseline applies.
*   **Variances / Final Expenditures:** The actual total spent at completion, and whether it includes contingency drawn or reserve released.
*   **Variances / Cost Variance:** The difference between the planned and actual cost, in currency and as a percentage, and the reason for it.
*   **Benefits Management / How Benefits Were Achieved:** Describe how the final product, service, or result achieved the benefits the project was undertaken to address. Identify the benefit and the evidence that it was realised, distinguishing a benefit that was realised from one that is still expected.
*   **Benefits Management / Benefits Realisation Status:** The current realisation status of each benefit, and who owns tracking it after the project team has been released.
*   **Business Needs / How Business Needs Were Met:** Describe how the final product, service, or result achieved the business needs identified in the business plan.
*   **Business Needs / Needs Not Met:** Any business need that was not met, and what happens next now that the project is closed. A need left unstated here will be assumed met by whoever inherits the product.
*   **Risks and Issues / Significant Risks and Issues:** Summarize any significant risks or issues, or the overall risk exposure, and describe the response and resolution strategies used. Record what remained open at closure, because an unresolved item transferred silently is the one most often lost.
*   **Risks and Issues / Items Transferred:** Any item transferred to the organization or to operations, with the recipient named. An item with no named recipient has not been transferred.
*   **Procurement Closeout / Contracts Closed:** The contracts closed out, with the final contract value and the date of each closure.
*   **Procurement Closeout / Obligations Discharged:** The obligations, warranties, and service levels that have been met, transferred, or remain outstanding. Name who holds each one now.
*   **Procurement Closeout / Claims and Disputes Settled:** Any claim or dispute that was settled before closure, and any that was not. An unresolved claim is a liability the project has just handed on.
