Context and Definition:
A request for proposal invites suppliers to compete for work, and it is the
document that makes the competition contestable. It says what is being bought,
what the supplier must demonstrate, how the submissions will be handled, and
- this is the part that decides whether the contest was real - the criteria
each proposal will be scored against and the weight each one carries.

Two distinctions do most of the work here. The first is between evaluation
criteria and a justification: criteria published after the bids arrive are not
criteria, and no supplier can appeal to them because no supplier saw them. The
second is between clarification and amendment: an answer given to one supplier
is a private change to the requirement, which is why every answer goes to
every supplier.

It can receive information from:
*   The procurement strategy, which sets what will be bought and how
*   The source selection criteria, which the evaluation method must follow
*   The project charter, whose objectives the bought outcome has to serve

It provides information to:
*   The statement of work, which is drafted from the winning proposal and
    cannot contradict what was invited
*   The contract closeout report, which reads what was delivered against what
    was bought here
*   The vendor performance scorecard, which measures against requirements
    fixed at this point

Tailoring Tips:
*   Publish every answer to every supplier. One supplier's clarification is
    another supplier's change to the requirement, and a private answer is the
    mechanism by which a contest stops being fair without anybody deciding
    that it should.
*   Give every criterion a weight, and say what a high score looks like. A
    criterion without a weight is a criterion that can decide the outcome
    without deciding anything.
*   Close the question period before the submissions are priced. A question
    answered late is answered to whoever has already committed a number.
*   State what a price must include. A request that asks for a price without
    saying what is in it produces responses that cannot be compared, and the
    cheapest one is then the one that left the most out.

Alignment:
The request for proposal should be aligned and consistent with:
*   The procurement strategy, which decides what is bought and on what terms
*   The source selection criteria, which the evaluation method must follow
    rather than restate
*   The statement of work, which is drafted from the winning response and
    cannot contradict what was invited here

Section Instructions:
*   Invitation and Scope: what is being bought and who authorised the
    invitation, with the functional and non-functional requirements kept
    separate because a missing function and a missed performance target are
    different failures.
*   Process and Evaluation: how submissions are handled, and the scored
    criteria with their weights - the section that makes the contest
    contestable.
