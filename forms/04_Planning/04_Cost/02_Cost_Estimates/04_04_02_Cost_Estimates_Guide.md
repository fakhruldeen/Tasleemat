# Project Artifact: Cost Estimates

**Document Reference:** `PMO-04.04.02`

This document provides a comprehensive reference to understand the purpose and usage of the **Cost Estimates**.

---

### 1. What?
A quantitative assessment of the likely costs for resources required to complete the project activities. It itemizes the expected monetary expenditures for labor, materials, equipment, facilities, services, and contingency reserves.

---

### 2. Why?
It is the mathematical foundation of the project budget. Without accurate cost estimates, the project cannot secure appropriate funding, establish a viable cost baseline, or mathematically measure cost variances during execution.

---

### 3. When?
Prepared during the **PLANNING Process Group** (Process 7.2 Estimate Costs). It is developed progressively; high-level estimates are created early, and detailed estimates are refined as more scope information becomes available (rolling wave planning).

---

### 4. Who?
**Responsibilities:** Developed by the Project Manager with input from Subject Matter Experts (SMEs), resource managers, and procurement specialists.

---

### 5. How?
Calculate and document the costs for each identified activity or work package in a centralized table containing:
*   **ID & Resource:** What is being estimated.
*   **Labor & Physical Costs:** The raw monetary cost of human and physical resources.
*   **Reserve:** Any contingency padding added for identified risks.
*   **Estimate (Total):** The sum of labor, physical, and reserve costs.
*   **Basis & Method:** The mathematical rationale (e.g., "$50/hr x 40 hrs") and the technique used (e.g., Parametric, Analogous).
*   **Assumptions, Range, & Confidence:** The context surrounding the estimate's reliability.

---

### Tailoring Tips
• Cost estimates may include contingency reserves for risks.
• If considerations for the cost of quality, financing, or indirect costs are included, add them.
• Estimate costs at the level of accuracy and precision that suits your project needs.
• If using vendors, indicate the estimated cost and the type of contract to account for possible fees and awards.

### Alignment
The cost estimates should be aligned and consistent with the following documents:
• Assumption log
• Activity attributes
• Project schedule
• Resource requirements
• Project team assignments
