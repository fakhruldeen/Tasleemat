## Tasleemat Forms Guide
# Project Artifact: Variance Analysis

**Document Reference:** `PMO-06.04`

This document provides a comprehensive, professional reference to understand
the purpose and effective usage of the **Variance Analysis** in alignment with the
Tasleemat framework.

---

### 1. What?
A structured analysis report identifying variances, diagnosing root causes, and formulating corrective actions.

---

### 2. Why?
Enables timely diagnostic understanding of project deviations and provides defensible evidence for corrective actions.

---

### 3. When?
Conducted periodically or upon detecting threshold breaches during monitoring and controlling.

---

### 4. Who?
Prepared by the Project Manager with Project Controllers and reviewed by PMO and Governance committees.

---

### Tailoring Tips
*   Set custom variance tolerance thresholds (e.g., +/- 5% cost or schedule) to trigger formal root-cause analysis.
*   Combine with Earned Value forecasting for capital-intensive infrastructure and engineering projects.

---

### Alignment
Interlinks with Project Management Plan baselines, Work Performance Reports, Earned Value Analysis, and Change Log.

---

### 5. How?
To accurately and professionally complete the **Variance Analysis**, the responsible party
must populate the following critical sections based on the project context (ensure `parameters.md` is referenced for global project variables):

*   **Schedule / Planned Result:** The target baseline schedule milestone, duration, or date.
*   **Schedule / Actual Result:** The actual completed milestone date, duration, or current progress.
*   **Schedule / Variance:** Calculated difference between actual schedule performance and the baseline.
*   **Schedule / Root Cause:** Primary cause and contributing factors for schedule variance.
*   **Schedule / Planned Response:** Action plan or recovery strategy designed to address schedule variance.
*   **Cost / Planned Result:** The approved baseline budget or planned expenditure for the period.
*   **Cost / Actual Result:** The actual expenditures or costs incurred during the period.
*   **Cost / Variance:** Calculated difference between actual expenditures and the cost baseline.
*   **Cost / Root Cause:** Primary drivers and events resulting in cost overruns or savings.
*   **Cost / Planned Response:** Corrective measures and cost containment actions to realign with budget.
*   **Quality / Planned Result:** The quality metric, acceptance threshold, or standard expected.
*   **Quality / Actual Result:** The actual measured quality outcome, defect rate, or test score.
*   **Quality / Variance:** Discrepancy between actual quality achievements and defined quality metrics.
*   **Quality / Root Cause:** Root cause analysis detailing why quality requirements were not met.
*   **Quality / Planned Response:** Remediation plan, rework procedures, or process improvements.
*   **Scope / Planned Result:** The authorized deliverables and work packages committed in the scope statement.
*   **Scope / Actual Result:** The actual scope deliverables completed and verified during the period.
*   **Scope / Variance:** Extent of scope variance, unapproved scope additions, or unfinished deliverables.
*   **Scope / Root Cause:** Factors causing scope variance, creep, or delayed deliverable completions.
*   **Scope / Planned Response:** Corrective actions, change requests, or realignment to baseline scope.
*   **Implications of continued variance (Forecast):** Anticipated long-term impact on overall project objectives if variances persist.
*   **Escalation Required (Y/N):** Clear indication (Yes/No) whether senior leadership escalation is necessary.
*   **Escalation Details:** Details of the specific escalation path, required decisions, and target stakeholders.
*   **Comments:** Additional explanatory notes, cross-functional dependencies, or contextual insights.
