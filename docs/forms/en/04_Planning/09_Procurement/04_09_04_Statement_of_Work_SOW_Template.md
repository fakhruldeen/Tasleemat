Context and Definition:
A statement of work is the document that turns an agreement to buy something
into an obligation somebody can be held to. It says what will be delivered,
by when, to what standard, how acceptance will be decided, and - this is the
part most forms leave out - who may change it and how.

The distinction that matters is between a statement of work and a proposal. A
proposal describes work someone has offered to do. A statement of work
describes work someone has agreed to do, states the authority that agreed
it, and gives the parties a route to change it without either of them having
to break the contract. A document without the authority clause and the
change route is a proposal that has been filed in the wrong place.

It can receive information from:
*   The procurement strategy, which sets what will be bought and how
*   The project charter, whose objectives the work has to serve
*   The requirements documentation, so the statement of work describes the
    same scope the project is managing against

It provides information to:
*   The contract closeout report, which reads what was actually delivered
    against what was agreed here
*   The product acceptance form and the vendor performance scorecard, which
    both measure against the acceptance criteria set here
*   The procurement audit, which needs the agreed terms to test conformance

Tailoring Tips:
*   Write what is excluded. An omission reads as an inclusion, and the
    argument about scope almost always starts with something nobody said.
*   Name the standard rather than describing it. A named standard can be
    tested and can fail; "best practice" cannot fail and therefore settles
    nothing.
*   Give the acceptance clause a decision maker and a deadline. Acceptance by
    silence is not a safeguard, because silence is what happens when nobody
    was ever given the obligation to decide.

Alignment:
The statement of work should be aligned and consistent with:
*   The procurement strategy, which decides what is bought and on what terms
*   The requirements documentation, so the scope described here is the scope
    the project is managing
*   The change request and change log, because the change route written here
    is the one those records follow

Section Instructions:
*   Scope and Deliverables: what will be delivered, by when, to what standard,
    and how acceptance will be decided, with the exclusions and the authority
    that agreed it stated rather than implied.
*   Terms and Governance: who is accountable on each side, what both parties
    are assuming, and how a change gets in - the parts that decide whether
    the document governs the period of performance or is read once at
    signature.
